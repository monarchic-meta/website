# Native source recreations

Source-led graphic studies for the [Framer visual kitchen](../../framer-moodboard.md).
They recreate selected observed motifs as native Framer frames and text on
1600 × 1000 artboards. Their named parts can be selected, moved, recolored,
resized, or regrouped individually. They contain no embedded SVG or raster
artwork. All 20 saved studies were confirmed in the Framer editor and remained
after a reload. The [manifest](manifest.json) records their saved Framer node IDs
and native layer counts.

Four Creative Coding studies, RE-17–20, are saved in a fifth row.
They are **source-led adaptations** with independently constructed geometry,
clearly credited to the references they study. They use static native layers;
they do not reproduce source animation, shader rendering or live interactions.
Their initial 1,514 recipe parts were verified after reload: 1,481 frames and 33 text
nodes, or 1,485 frames including the four artboard roots. The earlier 16 card
trees and all 77 source-reference trees were unchanged in that initial addition. Copied auto-height
text bounds were checked against the actual native Style panel dimensions.

These **RE-01–16** studies belong in **Source inspiration**, alongside the
comparison library. They are recreations of credited source work, not Monarchic
original compositions or approved branding. They sit alongside **18 independent
MB asset studies**. MB-19–24, including the source-led painting adaptation, have
been removed from the retained asset set at the owner's request. The 18 remaining
MB assets retain their existing artwork and identifiers;
the existing website prototypes are preserved.

## Source comparison refinements — 2026-10-29

The source review identified eleven native studies for refinement: RE-02, 03,
05, 07, 08, 13, 14, 15, 18, 19 and 20. The changes restore outlined SP-1
background lettering, the repeated Nucleus sheet, twisted wire ribbons, touching
staircase cubes, folded cyan petals, the source hero wording and interface
details, an elongated cobalt cloud, fine orange streams, and more faithful
canopy and corona silhouettes. The manifest retains each source key and records
the remaining simplifications. The other nine recipes are retained.

The comparison uses original images and sampled source-video frames. Native
RE-18 and RE-20 remain static, reduced approximations; their live counterparts
provide procedural motion and luminous detail. This review does not establish
source-algorithm equivalence or full-clip motion fidelity.

All eleven refinements were visually inspected in the Framer editor after a
reload. A separate fresh-main read matched every refined layer and child order
to the compiled recipes, with card, artwork and credit IDs retained. All 20
artwork layer counts matched; the updated notes matched their recipes and fit
inside their cards. The current set contains 6,770 recipe frames and 133 text
nodes, plus 20 artboard roots. The four Design pages, source assets, original
compositions and live-study controls were retained.

## Edit and hand off

In Framer, expand a study's named groups in Layers and edit its component parts.
Keep source keys, posting-account credits, and simplification notes with
duplicates. Source wording, identities, and metrics inside the artwork belong
to the reference; they do not become Monarchic product claims.

Each `.mjs` exports its ID, name, source key, background, simplification notes,
and `build(h)` construction. New source adaptations also export their explicit
`classification`; the compiler retains the recreation default for RE-01–16.
The notes identify reduced mesh or dot density,
font substitutions, static motion frames, omitted textures, and intentional
cropping. [recipe-tools.mjs](recipe-tools.mjs) compiles the manual authoring
recipes into native Framer layer descriptions.

These files are authoring and handoff sources, not site runtime components or
automatic Framer synchronization. Changes in either direction require an
explicit manual authoring step under the [Framer workflow](../../framer-prototyping.md).
