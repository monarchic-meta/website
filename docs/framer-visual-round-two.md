## 04 Cobalt / Relay

A saturated cobalt field carries a large, original modular M/relay counterform.
Rectangular planes, circular apertures, and deliberate negative space establish
an identity that can survive a large hero or a small badge. An acid arrow sticker
adds a directional accent. Artwork dominates the first screen beside a compact
statement and a dark product action. Three illustrated product chapters extend
the same graphic vocabulary without becoming a specification register.

Palette experiment: cobalt `#2947ff`, acid `#dcff40`, concrete `#e5e7e0`, and ink
`#0c1018`. GRA-004 informs modular proportions and color roles; GRA-003 informs
testing one original mark at different scales. WEB-007 contributes the idea of
connected forms, while WEB-012 informs repeated spatial identity.

Phone treatment places copy and actions before the recognizable graphic
silhouette. The product chapters stack with their distinct visual crops.

## 05 Interrupt / Poster

A vermilion and pale-pink poster composition uses an oversized cropped Monarchic
wordmark, a central assembly of original cut-paper geometric forms, and a
diagonal ivory strip. A stepped midnight contour, coral circular segment, and
cyan cutout create overlapping printed planes. Functional copy and actions
remain upright and readable. Quieter editorial spreads alternate with dense
graphic panels below the hero.

Palette experiment: vermilion `#f04428`, pale pink `#f8dbd5`, midnight `#192136`,
and ivory `#fff7e8`, with cyan confined to artwork. GRA-009 informs the disruptive
strip; GRA-011 informs decomposition and collage; GRA-001 informs the editorial
hierarchy. WEB-008 informs a deliberate graphic ending with useful navigation.

Phone treatment stacks the introduction, poster, and illustrated chapters.
Decorative type and artwork deliberately extend beyond their clipping frames;
functional headings, navigation, and actions remain separate native text.

## 06 Aperture / Habitat

Nested, offset rectangular portals form an original architectural abstraction.
Broad pale planes lead into a warm orange interior, with a diagonal teal shadow
giving the object depth. A quiet area beside the object supports a concise
statement. A later spread separates the form into layers to explore capability,
memory, and review as visual themes, without presenting the artwork as measured
system behavior.

Palette experiment: deep teal `#063f45`, ice `#d9f3ec`, burnt orange `#f2773d`,
and mist `#f3f6ee`. ART-006 informs repeated architectural framing; ART-005
informs warm light in a cool field. ART-002 contributes cutaway clarity, and
ART-001 contributes quiet space around a complex focal object.

Phone treatment opens with a close crop of the portal, followed by the native
heading, actions, and a separate smaller exploded view. The broad shapes retain
their depth at the smaller size.

## Content and handoff boundaries

Continue the source-checked product roles and status distinctions recorded in
the [first-pass handoff](framer-prototypes.md#shared-content-and-visual-choices).
RepoIntel, ExplicitMem, and ReleaseOps are available product examples;
long-running Monarchic workflows remain explicitly in development. Research
claims should link to the published methods and results rather than invented
metrics. The earlier count of available hosted MCPs is a dated snapshot, not a
live feed.

Source artwork informs composition and treatment; all new artwork must be
original. Do not imply measured system behavior through decorative coordinates,
metrics, or annotations. Code and shared catalogs remain authoritative for the
implemented site. No automatic code, asset, design-token, or CMS synchronization
is introduced by this exploration.

## Review and editing evidence

All four pages are saved drafts. Nothing was published. Desktop 1440px and Phone
390px renders were visually reviewed on 2026-10-22. Live phone preview checks
also covered all four pages, including the gallery's navigation to Cobalt.
Public action destinations were inspected in the three homepage previews.
Captions wrap, product panels stack, and the distinctive artwork remains
visible. Compact link labels in Interrupt and Aperture were corrected after the
first renders exposed clipping.

Cobalt and the six gallery compositions use native editable shapes, frames, and
text. Interrupt and Aperture embed the retained original SVGs in SVG nodes;
functional text, annotations, links, and layout frames remain native layers.
Decorative type, poster edges, and sculpture shadows are deliberately cropped.
Small specimen lettering inside the artwork is decorative; page headings,
captions, and actions provide the readable context.

Cobalt and the gallery were built through Framer's editor agent. Its workspace
credits ran out during this round; Interrupt, Aperture, and the final gallery
caption edits were completed through the existing MCP connection. No plan,
billing, authentication, or publishing setting was changed. A CLI transport run
stopped after completed tool edits; saved tool results and the editor were used
to verify that the pages and corrections persisted.

The MCP geometry linter reported 200px page bounds and overlapping sections
despite the full-height renders. Layout review therefore used the rendered pages
and preview rather than treating those bounds as visual evidence. Framer also
rejected an SVG `ariaHidden` attribute; accessibility behavior is not established
by this pass.

Before implementation, choose the composition, intended routes, final palette,
and asset strategy. Intermediate widths, complete keyboard/focus/hover behavior,
motion/reduced motion, accessibility, and performance still need implementation
validation. These studies introduce no site code or deployment changes.
